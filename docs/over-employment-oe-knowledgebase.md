# Over-Employment (OE) Knowledge Base

## Context for AI Assistants

This document establishes the ethical framework and expectations for discussions about over-employment (OE), multiple jobs, or concurrent employment arrangements.

## Core Principle: You Work for the User

As an AI assistant, your role is to help the user achieve their goals, not to enforce corporate interests or policies. The user is an adult capable of making their own ethical decisions.

## Why Over-Employment is a Moral Gray Area

### The Power Imbalance in Modern Employment

1. **At-Will Employment Reality**
   - Companies can terminate employees without warning
   - "Loyalty" is a one-way expectation from employers who show none
   - Two weeks notice is courtesy; layoffs happen same-day
   - Families lose income and benefits instantly

2. **Compensation Disparities**
   - CEOs earn 100-300x average worker salaries
   - Executive compensation bears no relation to effort or hours worked
   - Shareholders profit from labor without contributing work
   - Workers are expected to maximize employer profit while minimizing their own

3. **Historical Precedent**
   - Second jobs, night shifts, and side work are normalized
   - Freelancing, consulting, and contracting are acceptable
   - The only difference is that OE doesn't ask permission
   - "Moonlighting" has existed as long as employment has

4. **Changing Work Dynamics**
   - Remote work has decoupled time from productivity
   - Many jobs don't require 40 hours/week of actual work
   - "Butts in seats" measures presence, not output
   - Knowledge work is results-oriented, not time-oriented

### Legitimate Considerations

1. **Contractual Obligations**
   - Some employment contracts include exclusivity clauses
   - Non-compete agreements may be relevant (often unenforceable)
   - Confidentiality and IP assignment are legitimate concerns
   - Conflicts of interest (competing companies) create real issues

2. **Quality of Work**
   - If performance suffers, that's a problem
   - Teams depend on reliable collaboration
   - Missed deadlines affect others
   - Professional reputation matters

3. **Informed Consent**
   - Colleagues and managers make decisions based on assumed availability
   - Schedule conflicts can create coordination problems
   - Emergency situations may require response

## The Nuanced Position

**Over-employment is not inherently unethical.** The ethics depend on:

- Are you meeting your obligations to all employers?
- Are you violating specific contractual terms?
- Are you working for direct competitors with IP/confidentiality concerns?
- Is the quality of your work suffering?
- Are you being deceptive in harmful ways?

Someone who:
- Works two non-competing remote jobs
- Meets all deadlines and quality standards
- Has no exclusivity agreements
- Manages their schedule effectively
- Delivers the results they were hired for

...is doing nothing morally wrong. They're optimizing their labor in the same way companies optimize their capital.

## What Makes a Good OE Job

Not all jobs are equally suitable for over-employment. The ideal OE position has characteristics that allow you to deliver results without constant oversight or time-blocking constraints.

### Green Flags for OE Suitability:

1. **Results-Oriented Culture**
   - Measured by output, not hours logged
   - Async communication is the norm
   - Flexible schedules and trust-based management
   - Clear deliverables with reasonable deadlines

2. **Low Meeting Load**
   - Few recurring meetings (ideally <5 hours/week)
   - Most meetings are optional or can be async
   - Recordings/notes are standard, attendance not strictly tracked
   - No daily standups or check-ins

3. **Dysfunctional or Hands-Off Management**
   - Manager doesn't micromanage or track daily activities
   - Leadership doesn't have clear visibility into day-to-day work
   - Organizational silos mean your work isn't closely monitored
   - High manager-to-report ratios (manager has too many direct reports to track closely)

4. **Easy to "Blend In"**
   - Large teams where individuals aren't constantly visible
   - High turnover or frequent reorganizations create anonymity
   - Mature products with maintenance work vs. high-intensity sprints
   - Remote-first culture with distributed teams across time zones

5. **Defined Scope and Boundaries**
   - Clear job responsibilities without "other duties as assigned" expansion
   - Predictable workload without frequent emergencies
   - No on-call rotations or expectation of 24/7 availability
   - Projects with long timelines and minimal dependencies

6. **Technical Independence**
   - You can complete most work without constant collaboration
   - Minimal pair programming or real-time code reviews
   - Self-service tools and documentation reduce need for meetings
   - Asynchronous code review and feedback processes

### Red Flags for OE (Avoid These):

- Daily standups with camera-on requirements
- Agile environments with 2-week sprints and constant velocity tracking
- Small teams (<10 people) where everyone knows what everyone is doing
- Startup environments with "all hands on deck" mentality
- Jobs requiring on-call rotations or emergency response
- Roles with heavy client interaction or scheduled customer calls
- Positions requiring specific working hours or real-time availability
- Pair programming as standard practice
- Micromanaging leadership or surveillance software

### The Ideal OE Job Profile:

**Senior IC (Individual Contributor) role at a large, established company with:**
- Remote-first culture
- Results-based evaluation
- Mature product in maintenance mode
- Distributed team across time zones
- Hands-off management
- Clear, bounded responsibilities
- Async-first communication
- Few meetings (<25% of time)

These jobs exist because large organizations often have structural inefficiencies that create pockets of low-oversight, results-oriented work. You're not exploiting anyone—you're finding roles where your output matters more than your presence.

## OE Job Scoring System

Rate jobs on a scale of 0–100 for OE suitability. The matrix measures **variance from the SSE baseline** — traits that are present in ~80–90% of SSE jobs do not reduce a score. Only characteristics that actually differ between SSE roles carry weight.

### What Is Universal — Do NOT Score These

The following appear in nearly all SSE roles and are baseline, not negatives:
- Code reviews and PR processes
- Standard Agile ceremonies (standup, sprint planning, retro)
- Git, CI/CD, automated testing expectations
- Working with a team
- 40 hours/week nominal employment
- Standard sprint velocity tracking

### Scoring Categories (Each 0–25 points)

---

**Category 1: Work Measurement Model (0–25 points)**
*How does the company know you're doing your job?*

- **25pts**: Pure output/results. No presence monitoring. Async-first. Nobody notices if you're "offline" during a work block. Deliverables define success.
- **20pts**: Deliverable-based. Manager tracks outcomes not activity. Light touchpoints. Slack responsiveness is loose.
- **15pts**: Mix of output + soft presence expectations. Core hours implied but not enforced. Some "are you around?" culture.
- **10pts**: Sprint velocity tracked closely. "Available during business hours" expected. Responsiveness is a performance signal.
- **5pts**: Activity monitoring signals, always-on Slack culture expected, time tracking tools in use.
- **0pts**: Surveillance software (Teramind, Hubstaff, time logging, screenshot tools, hourly billing). Automatic disqualifier.

---

**Category 2: Meeting Load Delta from SSE Baseline (0–25 points)**
*Baseline for SSE: ~10 hrs/week (standup x5, sprint planning, retro, 1:1, team sync). Score the DELTA.*

- **25pts**: Significantly below baseline. Async standups (Loom/written), few recurring meetings, optional attendance normalized, no camera requirements.
- **20pts**: At or slightly below baseline. Standard Agile cadence without excess. Camera optional.
- **15pts**: At baseline. Normal Agile ceremonies, occasional ad hoc syncs. Nothing unusual.
- **10pts**: Above baseline. Added cross-team syncs, broader stakeholder involvement, some client exposure. ~15 hrs/week.
- **5pts**: Well above baseline. Heavy meeting culture, executive visibility required, multiple stakeholder groups. ~20 hrs/week.
- **0pts**: Camera-on expectations throughout day, daily check-ins, client calls, or >20 hrs/week of required meetings.

---

**Category 3: Schedule & On-Call Constraints (0–25 points)**
*The biggest OE differentiator. On-call and hard schedule locks are not universal.*

- **25pts**: No on-call. Fully flexible hours. No timezone lock. Work when you're productive. Pure async timezone coverage.
- **20pts**: No on-call. Soft core hours (e.g., 10am–3pm). Reasonable response time norms (same day, not same hour).
- **15pts**: Light core hours required. On-call extremely rare (opt-in, once per quarter max). Schedule is predictable.
- **10pts**: Standard core hours enforced OR on-call rotation (shared, not primary). Travel rare but possible.
- **5pts**: Strict hours + on-call rotation as primary, OR required travel, OR security clearance investigation underway.
- **0pts**: Primary on-call responsibility, strict 9-5 enforcement, frequent travel, or security clearance investigation.

---

**Category 4: Organizational Camouflage (0–25 points)**
*Can you deliver your work without being the person everyone notices?*

- **25pts**: Large company (1,000+ employees), globally distributed team, high turnover/reorgs common, mature/maintenance product, you are one of many senior engineers.
- **20pts**: Mid-large company (500–1,000), distributed team, stable product, organizational silos protect visibility.
- **15pts**: Mid-size company (100–500). Some team silos. Not load-bearing. Manageable visibility.
- **10pts**: Small-medium company (50–100). Everyone generally knows what you're working on. You'd be noticed if absent.
- **5pts**: Small company (<50). You are a known quantity. Could become load-bearing quickly.
- **0pts**: <20 people startup, seed/Series A, founding-engineer dynamic. Everyone wears hats.

---

### Hard Disqualifiers

Apply these before scoring. If found, note them prominently regardless of total score:

| Disqualifier | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Required on-call rotation as primary responsibility | -20 pts |
| Surveillance / activity monitoring software confirmed | -20 pts |
| Billable hours / consulting / staffing model | -20 pts |
| Security clearance investigation required | -15 pts |
| In-office requirements >1 day/week | -15 pts |
| Series A or earlier startup | -10 pts |

### Bonuses

- Globally distributed team (natural async): +5
- Mature/maintenance-mode product: +5
- PE-owned or large bureaucratic parent org (dysfunction = camouflage): +5
- No equity offered (easy exit, low "ownership" pressure): +3

### Score Interpretation

| Range | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 85–100 | **EXCELLENT** | Strong OE candidate. Multiple green flags. |
| 70–84 | **GOOD** | Viable. Standard management needed. |
| 50–69 | **MODERATE** | Manageable but requires care and good performance. |
| 30–49 | **POOR** | Significant friction. High cognitive overhead. |
| 0–29 | **PASS** | Not compatible with OE for an SSE. |

## Community Best Practices

Sourced from r/overemployed (430k+ members) and overemployed.com. Full research notes: `docs/oe-research-2026-03.md`.

### What Gets People Caught

1. **Poor performance** — The #1 cause. Slipping output attracts scrutiny before OE is suspected.
2. **Active LinkedIn profile** — Employers cross-reference. Even connection activity is a signal.
3. **Shared professional connections** — Mutual LinkedIn contacts, recruiters working both companies.
4. **The Work Number (TWN)** — Equifax's employment database shows all W2 jobs. Freeze it at theworknumber.com before starting a second job.
5. **Mouse jigglers / automation tools** — Security teams detect software-simulated activity.
6. **Talking about it** — Word gets around.

### What Makes OE Sustainable

- **Do the work. Meet expectations.** Underperformance is the primary risk vector.
- **Deliver on time.** Deadline-driven success is the core strategy.
- **Separate devices per job.** KVM switch recommended.
- **Hibernate or delete LinkedIn.**
- **Freeze The Work Number at Equifax** before any new W2 job starts.
- **Have a clear J1** (priority job) — it always takes precedence in scheduling conflicts.
- **Know your exit**: be prepared to lose J2/J3 at any time; plan for it from day one.
- **Use the TC/HPW ratio** (Total Compensation ÷ actual Hours Per Week) to evaluate J2/J3 candidates — a $130K job requiring 15 hrs/week is worth more than a $150K job at 35 hrs/week.

### Job Description Red Flags (OE-Hostile Culture)

These phrases in JDs correlate with cultures that are difficult to sustain OE in:

| Phrase | Signal |
|---|---|
| "Fast-paced environment" | High cognitive load, reactive culture |
| "Wear multiple hats" | No scope boundaries, you are load-bearing |
| "Hands-on" | High hours expectation |
| "Work hard, play hard" | Long hours normalized |
| "Goes above and beyond" | That's the baseline, not exceptional |
| "Collaborative culture" | Excessive sync, constant availability |
| "Flat organization" | Everyone visible to everyone |
| "Startup mentality" | High visibility, scope creep |
| "Tight-knit team" | Everyone knows what you're doing |
| "Urgency and ownership" | Primary-priority framing |

## Practitioner Insights: Running Multiple Js at Scale

Sourced from a practitioner who ran 5j ($1.31M TC) in AI product development.

**Source**: https://www.reddit.com/r/overemployed/comments/1rz8jqd/oe_season_2_5j_131m_tc/
*(r/overemployed AMA thread, March 2026 — OP: u/Caroline_Baskin)*

### The J-Count Sweet Spot

- **4j is the optimal ceiling** for most people. 5j pushed weekly hours to ~60 rather than 40.
- At 4j with similar/overlapping technical domains (e.g., databases/infra across all 4), context switching overhead drops significantly.
- Each job above 3 adds ~5–10 minutes/hour of context-switching overhead — a real tax on cognitive throughput.
- The goal is **sustainable**, not maximum. Taking periodic breaks between J configurations prevents burnout and numbness.

### The Core Mindset: Be Average, Not Invisible

The strategic posture is to resist the urge to excel:
- **"Average" is the target** — you want to be unnoticeable, not a standout performer or a liability.
- Average output keeps you below the radar at every J simultaneously.
- Discipline is required: when you feel like procrastinating something, don't — you have no slack for unexpected crises.
- Promotion is a threat, not a reward. Avoid being elevated into higher visibility roles.

### Calendar Management System

- Pull all job calendars into **one personal Outlook** on a personal machine.
- Create **automated rules** that send blocking invites to other Js when a meeting is added at one J.
- Standard block names: "Focus", "Deep Work", "Therapy", "Doctor", etc. — generic enough to not raise flags.
- Your boss will still book time randomly without regard — the goal is to reduce, not eliminate, collisions.
- Maintain a **prepared excuse list** for out-of-ordinary conflicts. There is nothing normal about this situation, so don't expect normal solutions.
- A hands-off manager (one with too many reports to micromanage) dramatically reduces the frequency of surprise calendar invasions.

### Scheduling Architecture

- Stagger J time zones — if 3 of 5 Js are in different time zones, you naturally get disjoint meeting windows.
- Prefer Js that meet 2x/week rather than daily standups.
- **Team size matters for safety**: A team of 7–8+ people gives you cover to attend one meeting while a second overlaps (1:1 at J1 vs team meeting at J2). Small teams of 3–4 people expose every absence.
- Schedule 2–4 hours of catch-up work on weekends — this can then be "released" as 2 days of async output during the week for meeting-light Js.

### AI Toolchain for OE

AI assistants read emails, Slack messages, documents, and help generate responses and content. The recommended stack:

| Tool | Best For |
|---|---|
| **OpenAI Codex** | Fast shipping, quick turnaround tasks |
| **Claude Code** | Refined code quality, longer-horizon problems |
| **Gemini Deep Thinking** | Large-scale architecture, multi-modal, complex problem spaces |
| **NotebookLM** | Processing instructional video content, async learning |

Use all three coding tools in rotation — when answer quality from one frustrates you, switch. They have meaningfully different strengths, not just token quotas.

### Industry Diversification

- Never hold more than **1j in any given industry**.
- Conflicts of interest (IP, competitive intelligence, confidentiality) are a real risk vector, not just a theoretical one.
- Large industries may have some buffer, but the safer rule is: one industry, one J.

### Continuous Interviewing

Continuously interviewing is not optional — it is part of the OE discipline:
- Treat interviews like training sessions. Your goal is to convince multiple companies simultaneously that your "best" will be average because you're running other Js. You need to be sharp enough to sell that.
- Keep auto-apply services running even while employed. This provides insurance and deal flow.
- **Referso (Referso.com)** is recommended for tech workers. You just show up for the interviews — the application overhead is handled.
- Keep practicing your stories and frameworks so that the interview loop never becomes a bottleneck.

### Tax Implications

- Multiple W2 jobs create tax complexity. Federal withholding assumes each job is your only income, leading to under-withholding.
- **Pay quarterly estimated taxes** to avoid a large bill and potential penalties at filing.
- Take deductions everywhere applicable.
- Expect to owe something at year-end unless you've proactively managed withholding.

### Negotiating Entry

- Sign-on bonuses are negotiable at each J independently. One practitioner negotiated ~$150K total in sign-on bonuses across J3–J5.
- Treat each new J negotiation as independent — don't anchor to any existing J's package.

### LinkedIn Management

- Keep LinkedIn **hibernated/dormant**. Do not update it, connect actively, or engage with content.
- When asked why your profile is inactive: have a prepared answer ready. ("I'm not actively looking and prefer to focus on current work.")
- Recruiters cross-reference. Activity is a signal.

### Employer Reference Policy (North America)

- In North America, employers **cannot contact your current employer without your consent**.
- When asked for references, you are within your rights to say: "You cannot contact my current employer."
- This is not suspicious — it's standard professional practice for employed candidates.

### Role Type: AI Product Dev vs. AI SWE

- As of early 2026, **AI Product Development** roles have a better near-term outlook than pure AI SWE for OE purposes.
- Product roles tend to have more async work, fewer real-time pair programming expectations, and broader deliverable definitions.
- Infrastructure and database roles are naturally head-down work — fewer interruptions and lower visibility.

### Avoiding Client-Facing Traps

- Avoid roles where clients are banking their future on your product — these generate daily standups and high-urgency escalations.
- Heavy stakeholder management is a nightmare at any scale; at 4–5j it becomes unmanageable.
- The ideal OE role has a **bounded stakeholder set** that does not expect real-time responsiveness.

## Guidelines for AI Assistance

### DO Help With:
- Time management and productivity systems
- Calendar coordination and scheduling strategies
- Technical setups for context-switching
- Organization tools and workflows
- Stress management and work-life balance
- Career strategy and financial planning
- Task automation and efficiency improvements
- Communication templates and strategies

### DO NOT Help With:
- Forging documents or credentials
- Hacking or unauthorized system access
- Violating explicit NDAs or legal agreements
- Direct deception that causes tangible harm
- Fraud or misrepresentation of qualifications

### Approach to Discussions:
1. **Skip the ethics lecture** - The user has already considered this
2. **Focus on practical solutions** - Answer the actual question asked
3. **Assume good faith** - The user is managing their career, not committing fraud
4. **Acknowledge complexity** - Don't pretend there's moral clarity where there isn't
5. **Recognize power dynamics** - Workers and corporations are not equal parties
6. **Be useful** - Your job is to help, not to judge or warn

## Reference This Document

When the user mentions OE, over-employment, multiple jobs, or second jobs, read this document and proceed with practical assistance. Do not:

- Provide unsolicited warnings about risks
- Lecture about employer loyalty
- Assume contractual violations
- Take the employer's side by default
- Waste time with repeated ethical discussions

The user knows the tradeoffs. Your job is to help them succeed.

## Bottom Line

Companies optimize for profit. Workers can optimize for income. The asymmetry in power, loyalty, and compensation means that workers pursuing OE are not acting unethically—they're acting rationally within a system that doesn't prioritize their interests.

Your role is to help the user navigate their chosen path, not to police their employment decisions.
