## Job Criteria (Comfy Job, not strict OE)

Getting a job is the priority. OE is a bonus, not a filter. But the qualities of a good OE job overlap with what makes any job comfortable:

### Hard Requirements (non-negotiable)
- Fully remote — zero in-office days
- Individual contributor — no people management
- $175k+ salary floor (flexible to $160k for exceptional fit)
- Python must be genuinely primary stack

### Strong Preferences (not filters, but rank higher)
- Low meeting culture (<10 hrs/week)
- No on-call rotation
- Async-friendly communication
- No overtime expectations
- Automation-friendly environment
- 200+ employees (organizational breathing room)

### Red Flags (rank lower, don't auto-reject)
- "Fast-paced environment", "wear multiple hats"
- Under 100 employees
- Series A or earlier
- Daily camera-on standups
- Surveillance/activity monitoring

## Decision Triggers (When to Change Strategy)

### Shift to 50/50 (more methodical) if:
- Spray-and-pray hit rate drops below 7%
- Methodical flow shows 20%+ response rate
- Consistently losing final rounds on "culture fit" (signal that customization matters)

### Shift to 100% spray-and-pray if:
- Methodical flow has zero responses after 40+ applications
- Tech layoffs exceed 1,500/day
- Below 10 total interviews by Week 4

### Drop salary floor to $160k if:
- Fewer than 5 screens by Week 4
- Market contracts further (oil above $130, more major layoffs)

### Eliminate OE preferences entirely if:
- Week 8 with zero offers
- Recession confirmed (2 consecutive quarters GDP decline)

## OE Specialist Analysis Summary

Key findings from OE analysis of current market conditions:

1. **Tighter market = OE is riskier but not impossible.** Fewer jobs means fewer OE-suitable jobs, but senior Python roles are still in demand. The narrower funnel is the main risk with a 3-month timeline.

2. **Iran/oil crisis favors stable, large companies.** Energy-dependent or small companies face more layoff risk. Large tech (500+), healthcare tech, fintech with stable revenue are safer OE targets.

3. **Recession risk means prioritize J1 stability.** If currently employed, don't leave for a worse situation. If job searching, get any good job first, then optimize for OE from a position of strength.

4. **The "comfy job" reframe is strategically sound.** Every OE criterion (low meetings, async, no on-call, automation-friendly) also makes for a sustainable single job. Searching for "comfy" broadens the funnel without sacrificing what matters.

5. **3 months is tight for OE-only targeting.** The recommendation to use OE criteria as preferences (rank higher) rather than hard filters (reject) preserves the funnel while still prioritizing the right kind of roles.
