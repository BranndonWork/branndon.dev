# Interview Cheatsheet (1-Pager)

## Core Philosophy

This is case prep, not a date. Professional distance = confidence. I only need 1-2 offers. Volume wins.

## Current Reality

-   Market is tough - need proactive volume strategy
-   Target: Senior Software Engineer (not lead/architect)
-   Strength: Personable, honest, strong tech skills
-   Risk: Showing desperation as time goes on

## Daily Routine

**Morning (9am-12pm):**

-   Apply to 10-15 positions (template + 2-3 custom sentences)

**Afternoon (1pm-5pm):**

-   Interview slots

**Evening:**

-   Prep next day's interviews (15 min each: research company, review tech stack, prep 2-3 questions)

## 15-Min Pre-Interview Prep

-   [ ] Company's product (1 sentence)
-   [ ] Tech stack from job description
-   [ ] 2-3 questions to ask them
-   [ ] Review my "greatest hits" stories

## Interview Flow (Every Time)

1. **Small Talk** - Match their energy, don't exceed it
2. **Listen to Their Pitch** - What problem does hiring solve for them?
3. **My Background** - Use STAR stories anchored to their problem
4. **Technical Discussion** - Be honest, show problem-solving
5. **My Questions** - Ask 2-3 prepared questions
6. **Close** - "This sounds like a good fit" (NOT "I really want this!")

## Standard Questions to Ask

1. "What does success look like for this role in the first 6 months?"
2. "What's the biggest technical challenge the team is facing?"
3. "Why is this role open - new headcount or backfill?"

## Handling Common Objections

**"You seem overqualified"**
→ "I'm happiest as an IC focused on coding. I've done architecture/management and intentionally seeking senior engineering."

**"Why are you looking?"**
→ "I'm being strategic about my next role to ensure it's the right fit technically and culturally."

**"Why not lead/principal?"**
→ "I'm most effective hands-on coding. I prefer being deep in technical work."

**"Salary expectations?"**
→ "I don't have a specific number. Focused on finding the right fit - confident we can work out fair compensation."

**"Tell me about a failure"**
→ Use prepared story: failure → learning → better outcome. Keep it professional.

## My Elevator Pitch (30 sec)

"I'm a senior software engineer with [X years] building [type of systems]. Strong in [tech stack]. I've done architecture/management but I'm happiest as an IC shipping quality code. Looking for a role where I can dive deep technically."

## "Greatest Hits" Stories (Prepare Once, Reuse)

1. Technical challenge I solved
2. Team collaboration success
3. Failure → learning
4. Ambiguous project I owned
5. Technical decision I made

## Energy Management (Prevent Desperation)

**Before:** "I only need 1-2 offers. This is one data point."

**During:** If over-explaining → STOP, breathe, return to facts. Lean back physically.

**After:** Wait 24 hours, send brief thank-you, move on mentally.

## What to Hide vs. Share

**Be Honest About:**

-   Skills I don't have (but willing to learn)
-   Why I prefer IC over lead
-   What I'm looking for

**Don't Volunteer:**

-   How long I've been looking
-   Financial pressure
-   Past salary

## Mental Reminders

**Truth:** Market is tough. Time pressure is real.

**Frame:** Volume game. Each interview = practice. Better with each one.

**Mantra:** "I'm a strong senior engineer evaluating options. They'd be lucky to have me."

**Rule:** Desperation repels. Professional confidence attracts. Fake it till you make it.

## Success = 1-2 Offers in 4-6 Weeks

**Focus on what I control:**

-   10-15 apps/day
-   5-10 interviews/week
-   Quality performance

**Let go of what I can't:**

-   Offer rate
-   Timeline

Do the volume. The offers will come.
