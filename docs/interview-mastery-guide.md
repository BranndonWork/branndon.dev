# Interview Mastery Guide

## Core Philosophy

In the same way that I prepared for my court cases, I need to become an expert interviewee. The goal is not to make them like me, it's to get a job. I need to study their approaches, predict objections, and counter or prevent shutdowns. Basically, by putting myself in their shoes, respond with what they would want to hear - factually, of course, but without seeming desperate.

## Current Situation (Context)

- **Market**: Super tough right now - proactive and strategic approach required
- **Timeline**: Couple weeks without work - urgency is real but can't show it
- **Target**: 1-2 job offers through volume and efficiency
- **Experience Level**: Lead-level capable, but targeting Senior Software Engineer roles
- **Background**: Former application architect and manager, now prefer IC/coding only
- **Strengths**: Personable, honest, strong tech experience
- **Challenge**: Risk of appearing too eager as time goes on

## The Interview Routine (Repeatable System)

### Pre-Interview Prep (15-20 minutes per company)

**Research Checklist:**
- [ ] Company's main product/service (one sentence description)
- [ ] Recent news or funding rounds (last 6 months)
- [ ] Tech stack (from job description + Glassdoor/Blind)
- [ ] Interviewer LinkedIn profiles (if names provided)
- [ ] 2-3 questions about their product/tech to ask

**Mental Frame:**
- This is case prep, not a date
- I'm evaluating them as much as they're evaluating me
- I have options (even if I don't yet - act like I will)
- Professional distance = confidence

### The Standard Interview Structure

**Phase 1: Small Talk (2-5 min)**
- Be personable but measured
- Match their energy level, don't exceed it
- Smile, be friendly, but don't oversell

**Phase 2: Their Pitch (5-10 min)**
- Let them talk about the role/company
- Take notes (shows interest without eagerness)
- Identify: What problem are they trying to solve by hiring?

**Phase 3: My Background (10-15 min)**
- Use the STAR framework for stories (Situation, Task, Action, Result)
- Anchor to their problem from Phase 2
- Downplay lead/architect experience (position for senior IC)

**Phase 4: Technical Discussion (varies)**
- Be honest about what I know/don't know
- Show problem-solving approach, not just answers
- Ask clarifying questions (shows thoughtfulness)

**Phase 5: My Questions (5-10 min)**
- Always ask 2-3 questions (shows engagement without desperation)
- Focus on: tech stack, team structure, why the role is open
- Avoid: "Do I get the job?" energy

**Phase 6: Close (2-5 min)**
- Express interest professionally: "This sounds like a good fit"
- NOT: "I really want this!" or "When will I hear back?"
- Ask about next steps, timeline

### Handling Common Objections/Shutdowns

**"You seem overqualified"**
- "I've learned that I'm happiest as an IC focused on coding. I've done the architecture and management roles, and I'm intentionally seeking a senior engineering position where I can focus on the work I enjoy most."

**"Why are you looking?" (Unemployed question)**
- "I'm being strategic about my next role. I want to make sure it's the right fit technically and culturally, so I'm taking the time to find that."
- NOT: "I need a job" or "I've been looking for weeks"

**"Why senior and not lead/principal?"**
- "I've found I'm most effective and happiest when I'm hands-on coding. I've done the higher-level roles, but I prefer being deep in the technical work."

**"What are your salary expectations?"**
- "I don't have a specific number in mind yet. I'm focused on finding the right fit, and I'm confident we can work out fair compensation if this moves forward."
- Deflect until written offer (see salary negotiation guide)

**"We're looking for someone with more X experience"**
- "I understand. Can you tell me more about what specific aspect of X is most critical for this role?"
- Then bridge to similar experience I DO have

**"Tell me about a time you failed"**
- Have 1-2 prepared stories that show: failure → learning → better outcome later
- Keep it professional, not personal
- Show self-awareness without dwelling on it

### Energy Management (Preventing Desperation Creep)

**Before Each Interview:**
- Remind myself: I only need 1-2 offers, not every offer
- This is one data point in a volume game
- Professional distance = attraction (like dating, ironically)

**During Interview:**
- If I feel myself over-explaining or over-selling → STOP, breathe, return to facts
- If I notice eagerness creeping in → lean back physically, slow down speech
- Mirror their energy level, never exceed it

**After Interview:**
- No immediate follow-up email (wait 24 hours minimum)
- Short thank-you note, professional tone
- Then move on mentally to next interview

### The Numbers Game (Volume Strategy)

**Goal**: Get through as many interviews as efficiently as possible

**Daily Routine:**
- Morning (9am-12pm): Apply to 10-15 positions
- Afternoon (1pm-5pm): Interview slots
- Evening: Prep for next day's interviews (15-20 min each)

**Application Strategy:**
- Apply to senior roles at companies where my experience is a fit
- Don't overthink each application - volume matters
- Use cover letter template, customize 2-3 sentences per company

**Interview Funnel Math:**
- 100 applications → 10-15 phone screens → 5-7 technical interviews → 2-3 offers
- At 10-15 applications/day → 1-2 weeks to hit 100
- Need to be interview-ready constantly

### Positioning for Senior (Not Lead/Architect)

**Frame My Experience:**
- "I've worn many hats - architect, manager - but what I've learned is I'm most effective as a senior IC focused on shipping quality code."
- Emphasize: hands-on coding, technical depth, collaboration
- Downplay: leadership, architecture, management

**Red Flags to Avoid:**
- Don't mention how I "used to manage teams" unless asked
- Don't position myself as "I can also do architecture" - stay in senior lane
- Don't talk about business strategy - focus on technical execution

**What They Want to Hear (Senior IC):**
- "I love solving hard technical problems"
- "I enjoy collaborating with teammates"
- "I take ownership of my code and see features through"
- "I'm comfortable with ambiguity and can figure things out"

### Interview Artifacts (Prepare Once, Use Repeatedly)

**My "Greatest Hits" Stories (STAR format):**
1. **Technical challenge I solved** (shows problem-solving)
2. **Collaboration/team success** (shows I work well with others)
3. **Failure → Learning** (shows self-awareness)
4. **Ambiguous project I drove** (shows ownership)
5. **Technical decision I made** (shows judgment)

Prepare these once, use them across all interviews. Adjust details to fit their questions.

**My Standard Questions to Ask:**
1. "What does success look like for this role in the first 6 months?"
2. "Can you tell me about the team structure and who I'd be working with most closely?"
3. "What's the biggest technical challenge the team is facing right now?"
4. "Why is this role open? New headcount or backfill?"

**My Elevator Pitch (30 seconds):**
"I'm a senior software engineer with [X years] of experience building [type of systems]. I've worked across [tech stack areas], and I'm particularly strong in [1-2 key skills from job description]. I've done architecture and management work in the past, but I've found I'm happiest and most effective as an IC focused on hands-on coding. I'm looking for a role where I can dive deep technically and ship quality software."

### The Honesty Balance

**Be Honest About:**
- Technical skills I don't have (but show willingness to learn)
- Why I prefer IC over lead roles
- What I'm looking for in next role

**Don't Volunteer:**
- How long I've been looking
- How many other interviews I've had (unless it's a lot and going well)
- Financial pressure or urgency
- Past salary (see salary negotiation guide)

**The Rule**: Answer questions truthfully, but don't offer information that weakens my position.

### Red Flags I'm Watching For

Not every job is worth taking. I'm looking for RED FLAGS too:

- Disorganized interview process (reflects company culture)
- Unrealistic expectations for the role
- Toxic vibes from interviewers
- Unclear or constantly changing requirements
- Low-ball offers with no room to negotiate

**I'm interviewing them too. This is mutual evaluation.**

## Daily Checklist

**Morning:**
- [ ] Apply to 10-15 positions (use template, customize minimally)
- [ ] Respond to any recruiter emails (professional, brief)
- [ ] Prep for today's interviews (15-20 min each)

**Interview Day:**
- [ ] Review company research 10 min before call
- [ ] Have "greatest hits" stories ready
- [ ] Have standard questions ready
- [ ] Professional setup: quiet space, good lighting, test audio/video
- [ ] Water nearby, notebook ready

**Post-Interview:**
- [ ] Take 5 min to note: what went well, what to improve
- [ ] Wait 24 hours, send brief thank-you email
- [ ] Update tracker: company, stage, next steps
- [ ] Move on mentally - next interview is what matters

## Weekly Review

**Every Friday:**
- Review the week's interviews: what patterns am I seeing?
- Adjust pitch/stories based on what's resonating
- Check: Am I showing desperation? Adjust energy if needed
- Celebrate: How many interviews did I complete? (Volume is progress)

## The Mental Game

**Truth**: The market is tough. I've been out of work for a couple weeks. Time pressure is real.

**Frame**: I'm playing a numbers game. Each interview is practice. I only need 1-2 offers. The more I interview, the better I get, the higher my odds.

**Reminder**: Desperation repels offers. Professional confidence attracts them. Even if I don't feel confident, I can ACT confident by sticking to the routine.

**Mantra**: "I'm a strong senior engineer evaluating my options. This company would be lucky to have me."

(Even if I don't fully believe it yet, act like I do. Fake it till I make it.)

## Success Metrics

**Not success**: Getting every offer
**Success**: Getting 1-2 solid offers within 4-6 weeks through volume and efficiency

**Leading indicators** (what I can control):
- Applications submitted per day (10-15 target)
- Interviews completed per week (5-10 target)
- Quality of interview performance (measured by callbacks)

**Lagging indicators** (what I can't directly control):
- Offer rate
- Time to offer

**Focus on leading indicators. Do the volume. The offers will come.**
