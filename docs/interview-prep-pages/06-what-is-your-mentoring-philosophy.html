6. What is your philosophy on mentoring? (2 min)

  1. A good mentor spots a problem, says "I've seen this before, here's how we solved it" — and shows you what to do going forward instead of just pointing out that something is wrong.
  2. The best mentoring I've received came through PR reviews — not nitpicks, but someone pointing out a function that already did what I was reinventing, or a way to collapse three database reads into one. That kind of feedback made me genuinely better.
  3. At Headspace with 500 engineers, nobody knows a 10-year-old system end to end. You can't learn about those things unless someone is willing to take a moment and show you.
  4. I mentor the same way: I look for patterns, not one-off mistakes. If someone's variable naming is vague or they're duplicating something that already exists, I share the standard or the existing solution — not just a correction.
  5. At The Penny Hoarder I ran weekly code reviews and grew alongside developers from a 10-person team to 100+. At Headspace I mentored junior engineers and promoted best practices across the team.
  6. I want the person I'm mentoring to walk away knowing *why*, not just *what* — so they can make the right call on their own next time.

Key themes to hit:

  • Show, don't just tell: Point to the existing solution, don't only flag the problem
  • Context matters: Large systems mean nobody knows everything — mentors fill that gap
  • PR reviews as mentoring: Real-world example from Headspace
  • Track record: Penny Hoarder weekly reviews, Headspace junior engineers
  • Goal: Build judgment, not just compliance

"That's what I think a good mentor is — saying 'I see a problem where this could be better, I've seen this before, here's how we solved for it, and this is probably what you should do going forward instead of recreating it.'"
