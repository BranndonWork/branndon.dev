4. Tell me about a production mistake (STAR, 2 min)

  1. At The Penny Hoarder, I was leading the HTTP to HTTPS conversion when pages started loading without CSS intermittently - they'd render but look completely broken.
  2. I traced it to a CloudFront caching issue where certain iOS versions would cache pages with http:// stylesheet links, and whichever device hit first determined the cache for everyone.
  3. I fixed it by correcting the server-level cache before it reached CloudFront, ensuring all devices got proper HTTPS references, then invalidated CloudFront to clear the bad cache.
  4. Now I always test cross-device caching behavior before any protocol or CDN changes.

STAR breakdown:

  • Situation: Leading HTTP to HTTPS conversion, pages loading without CSS intermittently
  • Task: Figure out why some pages broken for everyone, others fine
  • Action: Traced to CloudFront caching issue, fixed server-level cache, invalidated CDN
  • Result: Issue resolved, now always test cross-device caching before protocol changes

5. Tell me about a critical production problem (STAR, 2 min)

  1. During Mental Health Awareness Month at Headspace, our therapy matching system was taking 8+ seconds under 3x normal traffic, and 23% of users were abandoning the flow.
  2. I led a small team to stabilize it quickly - we investigated and found complex unoptimized database queries and redundant calculations in the matching algorithm.
  3. I tackled the database optimization myself, rewrote Django queries to eliminate N+1 issues, added indexes that dropped query time from 6 seconds to under 1 second, while another engineer added Redis caching for therapist availability.
  4. The tricky part was working with product - the matching had 8+ factors adding computational complexity, so I presented data showing performance cost versus user value and we simplified to 5 core criteria with optional refinement.
  5. Within two weeks we got matching time down to 1.2 seconds, abandonment dropped to 8%, and the system handled peak load without issues for the rest of the month.

STAR breakdown:

  • Situation: Mental Health Awareness Month, 8+ second matching time, 23% abandonment rate
  • Task: Lead team to stabilize system quickly under 3x traffic load
  • Action: Optimized Django queries, added Redis caching, simplified matching criteria with product
  • Result: 1.2 second matching, 8% abandonment, system stable rest of month

6. What is your philosophy on mentoring? (2 min)

  1. A good mentor spots a problem, says "I've seen this before, here's how we solved it" — and shows you what to do going forward instead of just pointing out that something is wrong.
  2. The best mentoring I've received came through PR reviews — not nitpicks, but someone pointing out a function that already did what I was reinventing, or a way to collapse three database reads into one. That kind of feedback made me genuinely better.
  3. At Headspace with 500 engineers, nobody knows a 10-year-old system end to end. You can't learn about those things unless someone is willing to take a moment and show you.
  4. I mentor the same way: I look for patterns, not one-off mistakes. If someone's variable naming is vague or they're duplicating something that already exists, I share the standard or the existing solution — not just a correction.
  5. At The Penny Hoarder I ran weekly code reviews and grew alongside developers from a 10-person team to 100+. At Headspace I mentored junior engineers and promoted best practices across the team.
  6. I want the person I'm mentoring to walk away knowing why, not just what — so they can make the right call on their own next time.

Key themes to hit:

  • Show, don't just tell: Point to the existing solution, don't only flag the problem
  • Context matters: Large systems mean nobody knows everything — mentors fill that gap
  • PR reviews as mentoring: Real-world example from Headspace
  • Track record: Penny Hoarder weekly reviews, Headspace junior engineers
  • Goal: Build judgment, not just compliance

"That's what I think a good mentor is — saying 'I see a problem where this could be better, I've seen this before, here's how we solved for it, and this is probably what you should do going forward instead of recreating it.'"

Memorize this as your natural close.

8. Conflicting priorities (STAR, 2 min)

  1. While I was leading the SDUI onboarding flow migration at Headspace — a year-long initiative — a GDPR compliance requirement came in with a hard regulatory deadline that couldn't move.
  2. I was the person who knew both the onboarding systems and the GDPR data flows, so I couldn't hand it off cleanly. The SDUI work had its own timeline with downstream mobile teams depending on it.
  3. I sat down with both PMs to map out the actual hard blockers versus the flexible milestones. The SDUI work had a two-week window where mobile was in a code freeze anyway, so I used that to focus entirely on the GDPR implementation — data anonymization workflows and DSAR request handling — without losing real time on SDUI.
  4. Both shipped on time. The key was being upfront early about the conflict rather than trying to silently carry both, and finding the natural slack in one timeline to absorb the pressure from the other.

STAR breakdown:

  • Situation: Leading SDUI migration when a hard-deadline GDPR compliance requirement landed
  • Task: Deliver both without slipping either timeline
  • Action: Mapped hard blockers vs flexible milestones with both PMs, used mobile code freeze window to absorb GDPR work
  • Result: Both shipped on time; learned to surface conflicts early and find natural timeline slack
