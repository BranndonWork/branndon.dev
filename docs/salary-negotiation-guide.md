# Salary Negotiation Guide

## Context
This document contains proven strategies for job search and salary negotiation. These approaches have resulted in $100k+ improvements in compensation packages.

## Core Strategies

### 1. Finding Niche Roles with Rare Skill Combinations

Finding really niche roles where my combination of experience and SME is rare, but exactly what they want. This requires constant monitoring of job postings and what I'm looking for only posts a couple times a year. Basically a company is looking for such an unusual combination of skills and knowledge that the job description reads as if they want three different people. When the stars align such that I've been each of those three people at various points in my career, I hop on it.

**Implementation for Job Search:**
- Set up automated monitoring for job postings (LinkedIn, Indeed, specialized boards)
- Look for descriptions that seem to want "3 different people"
- Match against my multi-disciplinary background:
  - Software engineering expertise
  - Business automation/process optimization
  - [Add other relevant skill areas]
- Don't apply to generic roles - focus on rare combinations where I'm uniquely qualified
- These rare-match positions have much higher negotiation leverage

### 2. Hire a Professional Salary Negotiator

I hired a professional salary negotiator to ghost write all my comp correspondence (and give me live talking points for salary questions) and it made a world of difference. I've negotiated before, but only got another 10-20k over the initial offer. With the pro, I got an extra $100k. Completely worth it. She worked as an in-house recruiter for a big tech company as her day job and this was her side hustle, so she knew exactly what scripts made her HR execs sign off on exceptions and she gave those to me.

**Key Insights:**
- **ROI**: Previous negotiations: +$10-20k. With professional: +$100k
- **Cost-benefit**: Even at $5-10k for negotiation services, this pays for itself 10x over
- **Insider knowledge**: Look for negotiators who worked as recruiters/HR at target companies
- **Services provided**:
  - Ghost-written email correspondence for all comp discussions
  - Live talking points for phone/video salary conversations
  - Scripts that trigger HR approval for exceptions
  - Understanding of internal approval processes

**Finding a Negotiator:**
- Look for professionals with in-house recruiting experience at big tech companies
- This is often a side hustle for current HR/recruiting professionals
- They know exactly what language/framing makes executives sign off on exceptions
- Ask about their background and success stories

## Negotiation Process

### Before the Offer
1. Delay salary discussions as long as possible
2. When pressed, provide a range based on market research
3. Never reveal current salary (illegal to ask in many states)
4. Focus on value you'll bring, not what you "need"

### After the Offer
1. **Immediately engage negotiator** (if using professional services)
2. Always negotiate - initial offers have built-in room
3. Use competing offers as leverage (if available)
4. Negotiate total compensation, not just base salary:
   - Base salary
   - Sign-on bonus
   - Equity/stock options
   - Annual bonus structure
   - Benefits (PTO, remote work, equipment, professional development)
   - Relocation assistance
   - Title/level
